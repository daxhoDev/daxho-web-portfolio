## What it does

A portfolio for a self-taught photographer based in Holguín, Cuba, whose work is
mostly black and white. The whole site is bilingual — Spanish at the root,
English under `/en` — with no hard-coded strings in the components.

Three pages carry it. The landing opens with a full-screen photograph, a short
introduction and a selection of featured work, and ends in a contact form. The
about page holds the full biography with a portrait and a list of facts: where
she works from, since when, what she photographs, the equipment. The gallery
shows everything in a masonry layout with tone filters — all, black and white,
colour — and each photograph opens in a lightbox.

## How it is built

Astro with static output and strict TypeScript, styled with Tailwind CSS, fonts
self-hosted so no request leaves for Google, and images processed at build time
into AVIF and WebP.

**There is no UI framework**: no React, no Vue, no Svelte. What little
interactivity the site has — the filters, the lightbox, the language switch — is
plain TypeScript inside `<script>` blocks. That is what keeps the landing page
under 30 KB of JavaScript, the hand-written lightbox included.

Tests run on three levels: `node:test` for units and for the built output,
Playwright for the browser, and axe-core for accessibility across all seven
routes.

## The decisions that shaped it

The palette is achromatic: pure black, layered greys and a single accent chosen
by the photographer for rules and active states. Hierarchy is carried by
brightness, not by colour, precisely so that nothing competes with the
photographs. The only colours in the interface are the red and green of the form
messages, and neither travels alone: both come with text and an icon.

The photographs are never rounded, never cropped in the gallery and never
colour-graded by the interface. Motion is expressive, and it stops completely
under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
